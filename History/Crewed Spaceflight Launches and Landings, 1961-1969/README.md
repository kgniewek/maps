This map follows every crewed spaceflight from Yuri Gagarin's launch in April 1961 to Apollo 12's return in November 1969. Press play and the markers appear in date order, so you can watch the Soviet and American programs trade firsts: launches from Baikonur and Cape Canaveral, and landings on the steppe, in the forests of the Urals and in the Atlantic and Pacific oceans.

Each program has its own color and layer. Red to amber shades are the Soviet Vostok, Voskhod and Soyuz flights, and light to dark blue shades are the American Mercury, Gemini and Apollo flights, so the colors run from earlier to later within each country. Use the layer filter to show one program or compare the two sides.

Click any marker to see the country, who flew and what the flight achieved, and how exact the date is. Every flight has a launch marker, and the launch markers for one pad sit at nearly the same spot. Dates are to the day and use the UTC date, which can differ by a day from the local date.

The map covers crewed flights only, not satellites, probes or uncrewed test flights, and it does not show the Moon landings themselves because they are off the Earth. Launch pads are accurate to about a hundred metres. Landing positions are less exact: American splashdowns come from NASA records, while several Soviet landings are approximate and are noted as such. The landings of Soyuz 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 are missing because available sources disagree on where they came down.
