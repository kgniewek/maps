This map follows World War II from the Japanese attacks in China in 1937 through the German invasion of Poland in 1939 to the fall of Berlin and the surrender of Japan in 1945. When you press play, markers appear in date order, so you can watch the war spread from Poland and France to the Soviet Union, North Africa, the Atlantic and the Pacific, then close in on Germany and Japan. Long campaigns and sieges stay on the map for as long as they lasted.

Colors show seven groups. Blue is Western and Central Europe, red is the Eastern Front, orange is the Mediterranean and Africa, green is the Pacific and Asia, and purple is the sea war in the Atlantic. Slate gray marks occupation, the Holocaust and resistance, such as camp liberations, massacres, uprisings and partisan liberations, and sky blue marks conferences, agreements and surrenders. Use the layer filter to show only one of them.

Click a marker to see the country, the usual outcome of the battle, and how exact the date is. The outcome is the common historical summary, so a few, such as the First Battle of El Alamein and the Coral Sea, are marked inconclusive because the result was mixed.

This is a large selection of battles, sieges, air raids, operations and turning points, not every engagement of the war. Start and end dates are the ones most often given by historians, and some campaigns, such as the Blitz or the Battle of the Atlantic, have conventional rather than sharp boundaries. Coordinates mark the town, battlefield or landing area, and sea battles are placed at an approximate position.
