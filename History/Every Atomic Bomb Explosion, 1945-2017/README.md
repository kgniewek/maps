# Atomic Bomb Tests Every Nuclear Test Explosion, 1945-2017

Between **1945 and 2017**, nine decades of nuclear testing left more than two thousand explosions on the Earth's surface, under its seas and deep inside its rocks. This map shows where they happened, from the first test in the New Mexico desert to the most recent one in North Korea.

Click a marker to see the test, its date and the country that carried it out.

## The story in short

- **16 July 1945:** the United States fires the first nuclear device, *Trinity*, in New Mexico.
- **6 and 9 August 1945:** the bombs dropped on **Hiroshima** and **Nagasaki** are the only nuclear weapons ever used in war.
- **1949 to 1964:** the Soviet Union (1949), the United Kingdom (1952), France (1960) and China (1964) join the list.
- **1 March 1954:** the *Castle Bravo* test at Bikini Atoll, the largest US test, spreads fallout far beyond its planned zone.
- **30 October 1961:** the Soviet *Tsar Bomba* over Novaya Zemlya, the most powerful device ever exploded, at about 50 megatons.
- **1962:** the busiest year on record, with roughly 178 tests, about one every two days.
- **1963:** the Partial Test Ban Treaty moves testing underground and bans it in the air, in space and underwater.
- **1974 and 1998:** India tests in 1974 and again in 1998. Pakistan follows in 1998.
- **1990 to 1996:** the Soviet Union, the United States, the United Kingdom, China and France stop testing one after another.
- **2006 to 2017:** North Korea is the only country to test in the 21st century, six times in total.

## Good to know

- Most early tests were **atmospheric**: fireballs and fallout that drifted across borders. Later tests were mostly **underground**.
- Many test sites were on land belonging to Indigenous and island peoples, including the Marshall Islands, Kazakhstan, Algeria, Polynesia and Nevada. Communities there still live with the health and environmental effects.
- The **Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty** (1996) bans all nuclear explosions, but it has not yet entered into force.
