This map shows where organized higher learning took root, from the imperial school of Constantinople in 425 to the colleges and universities of Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas founded by 1700. Press play and the institutions appear in the order they were founded, so you can watch learning spread from the Mediterranean and Middle East into northern Europe and then across the Atlantic.

Each color is a century of founding, shifting from brown for the earliest schools through orange, green and teal to blue and purple for those founded after 1500. The layers are Before 1200, 1200s, 1300s, 1400s, 1500s and 1600s, and you can filter by any of them to follow one period.

Click a marker to see the country, a short note on how the institution began, and how precisely the date is known. All dates are shown as January 1 of the founding year because only the year is known.

Founding dates for medieval schools are often traditional or approximate, since many grew gradually from cathedral schools, mosque-schools or monasteries before receiving a charter. Some places, such as Salerno, Nalanda, Timbuktu and Ashikaga, are included with approximate dates or locations, and coordinates point to the city or main campus rather than the exact original building. The map is selective, not a complete list of every school.
